# Student User Guide: AI Learning Capstone

Welcome to the **Project 4 — AI Learning Capstone: Evidence-Driven Prompting & Self-Review** workspace!

This application is your structured workbench for building a short textbook chapter with the assistance of generative AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, etc.).

> **Important Reminder:** The actual generative AI chat remains *external* to this tool. This application serves as your scientific laboratory notebook where you direct, question, compare, correct, verify, and reflect upon every stage of the process.

---

## Quick Start: The 6-Step Workflow

### Step 1 — Define Your Topic Brief
1. Navigate to **Step 1 — Topic Brief**.
2. Enter your Name, Class/Audience level (e.g. *Grade 9 Biology*), and AI Tool Used (e.g. *Claude 3.5 Sonnet* or *ChatGPT*).
3. Select a focused learning topic (e.g., *Food chains / energy flow*, *Electric circuits*, *Fractions*, etc.).
4. Articulate a concrete **Learning Goal** (what the learner should master after reading your chapter).
5. All inputs save automatically to your browser. Click **Continue to Step 2**.

---

### Step 2 — Prompt Briefing (Three Stages)
1. **Stage 1 (Weak Prompt):**
   - Click **Copy Weak Prompt** (`Explain [TOPIC].`).
   - Paste it into your external AI tool.
   - Observe how generic or encyclopedic the default output is.
   - Paste the AI's response or your critical observations into the text box and click **Log to History**.
2. **Stage 2 (Context Prompt):**
   - Fill in your audience parameters, learner level, and pedagogical constraints.
   - Click **Copy Context Prompt**, run it in your external AI tool, and record the improved response. Click **Log to History**.
3. **Stage 3 (Source Prompt):**
   - Supply textbook excerpts or teacher notes.
   - Click **Copy Source Prompt** and run it in the AI.
   - **Crucial Requirement:** Identify and record points that are:
     - `SUPPORTED BY PROVIDED SOURCE`
     - `ADDITIONAL AI INFORMATION`
   - Log this interaction to your history.

---

### Step 3 — Options & Feedback
1. Click **Copy 3-Options Prompt** and submit it to your AI tool.
2. Review the three distinct pedagogical styles generated by the model.
3. Fill in the details for **Option 1**, **Option 2**, and **Option 3** (Explanation, Strengths, Weaknesses).
4. Click **Select as Preferred Approach** on your chosen option. The other two will automatically switch to **Rejected**.
5. Write your **Student's Decision & Rationale** explaining *why* you chose that approach and rejected the others.
6. In the **Decision Summary & Synthesis Outline** box, record how you will merge the best parts (e.g. combining Option 1's narrative with Option 3's misconception alerts).
7. Click **Log Feedback Prompt** to record this step.

---

### Step 4 — Chapter Builder
1. Click the **Ten Chapter Sections** tab.
2. You will see **Section 1** through **Section 10**.
3. For each section, enter:
   - A descriptive title (e.g. *Section 1: What is a Trophic Level?*)
   - Core conceptual explanation (in simple, accessible language)
   - Real-world examples
   - Common student mistakes to avoid
   - Revision bullet points
4. Click the **Flashcards** tab: Provide at least 2 active-recall Question & Answer cards.
5. Click the **Practice Quiz** tab: Create diagnostic multiple-choice questions with answer keys and explanations.
6. Click the **7-Day Revision Plan** tab: Fill out daily revision activities for Days 1 through 7.

---

### Step 5 — Review & Verification
1. **5A. Rubric Scoring:**
   - Score your chapter from 1 to 10 across four criteria:
     1. *Clarity*
     2. *Accuracy*
     3. *Age-fit*
     4. *Usefulness for revision*
   - For every criterion, provide an evaluative explanation and specify the **Smallest Useful Edit**.
   - *Remember:* A high rubric score does not mean facts are verified.
2. **5B. Fact Checker:**
   - Extract **6 to 10 important factual claims** from your chapter.
   - For each statement, choose a decision:
     - `Accept` (Supported by authoritative citation)
     - `Reject` (Incorrect assertion refuted by citation)
     - `Modify` (Partially true or oversimplified statement needing nuance)
     - `Needs checking` (Unverified statement requiring further research)
   - Provide the citation, textbook page, or empirical reason. If rejected or modified, supply the corrected text.
   - **Rule:** Never mark a claim as verified without explicit evidence!

---

### Step 6 — Process Notebook & Documentation
1. Navigate to **Step 6 — Process Notebook**.
2. Review the **Official Capstone Verification Checklist** to see which requirements are completed and which need attention.
3. Check the **Sources Table** tab: Ensure at least **2 named sources** (e.g. textbook, curriculum document, scientific journal) are recorded.
4. Check the **Prompt History Log** tab: Ensure at least **8 prompts** are logged across all stages.
5. Complete the **Student Learning Reflection**:
   - Write your comprehensive reflection essay (minimum 50 words).
   - Answer the 5 guided prompts. The word counter will confirm when the requirement is met.

---

## Completion & Export

When all 8 mandatory conditions are fulfilled:
- The status badge will change from **`CAPSTONE IN PROGRESS`** to **`CAPSTONE COMPLETE` (100%)**.

Use the **Export Actions Bar** at the bottom of the workspace:
1. **Download Notebook (.md):** Downloads the complete structured Markdown report to your computer.
2. **Copy as Markdown:** Copies the entire GitHub Flavored Markdown document to your clipboard.
3. **Print / Save as PDF:** Opens the browser print preview formatted for crisp PDF printing without UI clutter.
4. **Clear Project:** Resets the workspace with a safety confirmation prompt.
